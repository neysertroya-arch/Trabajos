The baseline cross-country analytical dataset contains 65 countries, of which 65 have complete covariates for the approved main specification. Table 1 summarises the distribution of post-census TFP growth, land-rental prevalence, farm structure, and development controls. The sample spans substantial variation in both rental intensity and farm structure. Figure 1 displays the unconditional cross-country relationship between rental prevalence and subsequent TFP growth, while Figure 2 presents the covariate-adjusted relationship implied by the approved linear specification.

In the main specification, a 10-percentage-point increase in the share of agricultural area rented from others is associated with a -0.0011 change in annualised post-census TFP growth (HC3 p = 0.598). The estimate is small relative to cross-country outcome variation and is not statistically distinguishable from zero at conventional levels. The farm-structure interaction is likewise imprecisely estimated (p = 0.449), providing no robust evidence that the rental-TFP association systematically varies with the national share of holdings below two hectares. Table 2 reports the underlying coefficients and HC3 inference.

The predefined robustness exercises do not materially change that interpretation. Using three-year post-census TFP growth yields an estimated 10-percentage-point rental effect of -0.0025 (p = 0.388); using pre-census five-year TFP growth as a diagnostic yields 0.0008 (p = 0.752); and removing observations flagged by Cook's distance yields -0.0001 (p = 0.956). These checks differ in purpose—outcome horizon, temporal diagnostic, and influence sensitivity—yet none reveals a stable positive association masked by the baseline specification.

Three additional checks reach the same qualitative conclusion. The alternative exposure based on the share of holdings rented from others produces an effect of -0.0014 per 10 percentage points (p = 0.586; N = 55). Defining the outcome as the acceleration of TFP growth after versus before the census produces -0.0013 (p = 0.730; N = 64). Finally, the repeated-census country fixed-effects specification uses 37 observations from 17 countries and produces an area-rental estimate of 0.0008 per 10 percentage points (p = 0.910). Because this within-country sample is small, the fixed-effects result is treated as exploratory robustness rather than as the principal estimate. Table 3 summarises these predefined checks.

Taken together, the estimates do not support a stable cross-country relationship between aggregate agricultural land-rental prevalence and subsequent national agricultural TFP growth in the available harmonised census sample. This is an associational result and should not be interpreted as evidence that rental markets have no productivity effects at the farm level or that rental-market reforms are ineffective.
