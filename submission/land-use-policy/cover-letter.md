Dear Editor,

We submit the manuscript **“Land Rental Prevalence, Farm Structure and Agricultural TFP Growth: Cross-Country Evidence from Agricultural Censuses”** for consideration as a Regular Paper in *Land Use Policy*.

The manuscript examines whether countries with a greater prevalence of agricultural land rental subsequently experience faster national agricultural total factor productivity growth. It combines harmonized structural indicators from FAO agricultural censuses with the USDA Economic Research Service International Agricultural Productivity database and World Bank development indicators. The analysis is explicitly associational and uses a reproducible R-based workflow with predefined robustness checks.

The manuscript contributes to the land-use and land-governance literature by distinguishing the **prevalence of rental transactions** from the **institutional and allocative effectiveness of rental markets**. While a substantial microeconomic literature shows that rental transactions can improve allocation under particular institutional conditions, the cross-country evidence in this study does not support rental prevalence by itself as a reliable predictor of faster subsequent agricultural TFP growth. We believe this result is relevant to *Land Use Policy* because it cautions against treating transaction volume as a sufficient policy-performance indicator and directs attention toward contractual security, transaction costs, market functioning, and the direction of land reallocation.

The authors confirm that the manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. All quantitative results can be reconstructed from the documented data and code pipeline. The source data are publicly available from FAO, USDA ERS, and the World Bank. An anonymized replication package can accompany the manuscript during double-anonymized review, with a public archival repository to be cited in the accepted version in accordance with the journal's research-data policy.

The authors have reviewed and take responsibility for the manuscript, its analyses, interpretations, and references. Use of generative AI during manuscript preparation is disclosed transparently in the manuscript in accordance with Elsevier policy.
