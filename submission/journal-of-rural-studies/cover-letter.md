We submit the manuscript **“Land Rental Prevalence, Farm Structure and Agricultural TFP Growth: Cross-Country Evidence from Agricultural Censuses”** for consideration as a Research Article in *Journal of Rural Studies*. The manuscript is being transferred through Elsevier's Article Transfer Service following editorial screening at *Land Use Policy*.

The study asks whether countries with a greater prevalence of agricultural land rental subsequently experience faster national agricultural total factor productivity growth. It combines harmonized structural indicators from FAO agricultural censuses with the USDA Economic Research Service International Agricultural Productivity database and World Bank development indicators. The design is explicitly associational and uses a reproducible R-based workflow with predefined robustness checks.

The manuscript speaks directly to rural social science by treating land rental not only as an agricultural transaction but also as a rural institution linking tenure arrangements, operational farm structure, household adjustment, and structural transformation. Its central contribution is to distinguish **rental-market prevalence** from **rental-market effectiveness**. Although farm- and household-level studies show that rental transactions can improve allocation under favorable institutional conditions, our cross-country evidence does not support rental prevalence by itself as a reliable predictor of faster subsequent agricultural TFP growth. This boundary result matters for rural policy because similar rental shares can arise under very different levels of tenure security, market depth, contract enforceability, and allocative performance.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. All quantitative results can be reconstructed from documented data and code. Source data are publicly available from FAO, USDA ERS, and the World Bank.
