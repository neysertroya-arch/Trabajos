Please consider our manuscript, **“Land Rental, Farm Structure and Agricultural TFP Growth: Cross-Country Census Evidence,”** for publication as a research article in the *European Review of Agricultural Economics*.

The paper examines whether national prevalence of agricultural land rental predicts faster subsequent agricultural total factor productivity growth. We link harmonised structural indicators from FAO agricultural censuses for 65 countries to the USDA Economic Research Service International Agricultural Productivity database and World Bank development indicators. The design is explicitly associational and uses a reproducible R-based workflow with predefined robustness checks.

The contribution is a cross-country boundary test of a mechanism well established in microeconomic land-market research. Although rental transactions can improve allocation when land moves toward more productive operators, our estimates show that aggregate rental prevalence by itself is not a reliable predictor of subsequent national agricultural TFP growth. The result is stable across alternative horizons, rental measures, influence checks, a growth-acceleration outcome, and an exploratory repeated-census fixed-effects specification. We therefore distinguish **rental-market prevalence** from **rental-market effectiveness**, a distinction relevant to agricultural economics, land-market policy, and the measurement of allocative performance.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have reviewed and approved the submitted version. Source data are publicly available from FAO, USDA ERS, and the World Bank.
